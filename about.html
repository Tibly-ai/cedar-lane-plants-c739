A quiet, unhurried approach to houseplants

Most indoor plants die from attention rather than neglect. Too much water one week, a sudden move across the room the next, a repot into a pot three sizes too big. Our whole method is built on keeping conditions steady so roots never have to recover from a surprise.

We started in 2016 with a cargo van, a moisture meter, and four accounts on a single street in East Nashville. The work has stayed the same since: arrive on schedule, water to the plant's need rather than the calendar, prune what is spent, and write down what we saw.

Our team trained in greenhouse production and interior horticulture, so we know the difference between spider mites and scale at a glance, and we treat early with the mildest thing that works. If a plant has reached the end of its life in a given spot, we will tell you plainly and suggest something better suited to that light.

What we believe

Light first, water second

Placement solves more problems than any feeding schedule. We measure the light in each room before we recommend a single plant.

How we work

The visit, step by step

Four things happen every time we walk in

Consistency is the whole product. These steps run in the same order at a two-plant condo and a forty-plant office floor.

  1. 01

    Walk the room

    Before anything gets watered, we look. Leaf color, new growth, drooping, any pot that has shifted out of its light. Problems announce themselves days before they get serious if someone is paying attention.

  2. 02

    Water to the plant

    Soil gets checked by finger and meter at depth, not at the surface. A ZZ plant in a dim corner and a bird of paradise by a south window never drink on the same schedule, so we never give them the same pour.

  3. 03

    Groom and treat

    Spent leaves out, dust wiped off, stems rotated toward the light. If we spot pests, we treat that day with horticultural soap or neem and flag the plant for a closer look on the next visit.

  4. 04

    Leave a note

    Every visit ends with a short written record: what we watered, what we pruned, what to watch. Over a year it becomes a useful history of how your space actually grows through Nashville's seasons.
